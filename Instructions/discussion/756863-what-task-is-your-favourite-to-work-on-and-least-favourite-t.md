**Zero** · 2026-11-08 13:21 UTC · 2 votes

I think this happens in almost every competition, not just this one.

When you first start working on a competition, breakthroughs often come quickly. But the further you go, the harder it becomes to make meaningful progress. And it's not just the people at the top who experience this. Even those of us who haven't reached your scores can feel the same thing.

So I think a competition tests more than just how well you understand the problem. It also tests your patience, and sometimes your willingness to look at the problem from a completely different perspective.

Recently, after my own progress stalled, I started focusing more on the mathematics behind the problems rather than just optimizing the circuits themselves. Of course, that might mean my score won't improve for quite some time.

But perhaps that's part of the challenge too. Sometimes you have to decide whether you're willing to bet on a fundamentally better breakthrough, knowing that the gamble might not pay off, and you could even lose the position you've worked so hard to reach.

But that's also part of what makes competitions so exciting, isn't it?
