- E1: `team/parity_tail_model.py` replaces the divider rows j ≥ j_tail of our exact integer model (and the final
  rounding digit) by parity rows on the full divisor d (p = [r ≥ 0], r' = 2r − d if p else 2r + d + 1, digit written
  as s = 1 − p in the incumbent's bit position), keeps every other stage, and refits the 34 angles with the same LP
  as `table_search.refit`, on the frozen v12 K = 7 table; the incumbent model under the same LP is the reference.

## Why

INV-B10's main risk was the error budget: a parity tail computes the quotient of (r0 + d + 1)/(2d + 1), off by a
factor (1 + 1/(2d)) on the tail value, which constant angles can only absorb on average.

- **GO**: twelve history-free rows cost 0.5% of the margin (criterion: keep ≥ 50%). Tails from rows 26 and 28 are
  even more accurate than the incumbent, because the incumbent truncates its divisor in rows 28–35 (its `Aa` rows)
  while this version A uses the full divisor.
- That is also the open cost question: the incumbent truncates those divisors to shorten its adders (fewer
  Toffolis). The Toffoli price of a full-divisor parity tail, and a truncated version B with nested odd divisors, is
  what E2 (scaled kickmix circuit) must measure. Overflow at tails 30/32 comes from mixing: truncated head rows
  leave the remainder outside the parity interval.
