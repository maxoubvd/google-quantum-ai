## What was done

Read Balram Srinivasan's notebook *VQCC 28.10: 41 cheaper GF(2^24) closings in Syed's v13 DQI* and his dataset
`balramsrinivasan/vqcc-28-10-norm-preserving-dqi-closings` (Apache-2.0). The idea: in
`gf24_nested_norm.emit_closing`, keep the 12-bit norm N alive and invert it through the GF(2^6) subfield,
N⁻¹ = conj(N) · C⁻¹ with C = N_6(N) (conjugation is free CX in the tower basis): 340 static Toffolis per closing
instead of 360, same 24 helpers and same action. v12 calls this closing 50 times (−1,000 static T, −0.26% task
cost, ≈ +0.09% score).

## Why deferred

The public form is a compiled gadget (`gadget/m_patch.kmx`) that a script transplants into a finished circuit;
there is no generator code. Our options: re-implement it in the generator (4–8 h, with the measured-inverse phase
repayment to redo exactly), or transplant his compiled fragment (1–2 h, but a third-party compiled piece inside our
circuit, at odds with decision S1.3). For +0.09% score, the user chose to defer it and move to invsqrt, where the
gains are larger. It stays in `IDEAS.md` as DEFERRED (re-implementation route).

## Results and analysis

No circuit change. Side note from his notebook: the v12-line failures (≈ 0.4 per 102,400 shots, matching our
0.35 per draw) come from the half-Euclid input classes at error weights 30–35; a cheap guard there (DQI-A07) would
be worth more than this closing (each avoided failed shot ≈ 0.15% score on the private draw).
